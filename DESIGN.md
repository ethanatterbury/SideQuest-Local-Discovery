# SideQuest design

SideQuest is a practical discovery workspace for choosing somewhere to go. Start with search, company, time and travel; show useful results immediately and provide direct access to the map and day planner. Keep family ages and further preferences available in context.

Use a near-white canvas, white surfaces, charcoal text and a restrained blue for primary actions and selection. Manrope headings and DM Sans controls keep the interface clear and compact. Fine borders and modest radii define groups without heavy decoration.

Use genuine place photography when available. A publisher-verified official logo may appear as a clearly labelled branding fallback; never call it a venue photograph. Otherwise use a short, neutral category row; never substitute unrelated imagery. Keep journey estimates, opening-hour uncertainty and admission checks visible. Category markers use familiar symbols, with clear selected and clustered states.

Preserve accessible labels, keyboard focus, reduced motion, mobile navigation and the map's expandable mobile sheet. Saved places, visits, collections, place details and planning share the same visual language and remain usable without an account.

Discovery presents six initial choices, with more available through an explicit action. Keep search drafts local until submission. Compact company, time, travel and activity controls lead; removable preference chips and a reset explain the current selection. Match summaries use the recommendation engine's evidence instead of separate promotional labels.

The day planner uses a photo timeline with arrival times, estimated connecting journeys and the journey home. Keep admission uncertainty separate from known costs, make children’s ages editable in context, and name an optional bring-your-own picnic honestly. Weather is one bounded procedural overlay: visible precipitation, independently drifting clouds, readable geography and static reduced-motion states. When WebGL is unavailable, degrade to a static tint.
